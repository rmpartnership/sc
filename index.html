How to use this scorecardQuick tips for reading the cards, labels and charts
  • Start with the three numbers above for the big picture.
  • Select a person in the milestone graphic, or a row in the Snapshot table, to open that milestone’s page. Each page starts with where we are now (the key number, its trend and the 2030 target), then explains what the data tells us, how we measure it, why it matters and what’s impacting it. Use Next, or the row of people at the top of each milestone page, to move between milestones.
  • Each card’s label shows at a glance whether a result is improving, not improving, holding steady, based on older data, or has no data yet. Changes in percentages are shown in percentage points (pts).
  • Use Search & filters at the top to compare specific metrics or show only ‘Big 3’ metrics.
  • Select Explore the data on a card for its full chart, exact numbers, notes and source, or open the Full progress table page to see every metric in one table.
  • Hover over (or tap) a chart, bar or asterisk (*) to see exact values and notes.

Tracking our progress towards 71,142

This target is not just a number. It represents our commitment to transforming lives and strengthening our community.

This work is RMP's contribution to StriveTogether's commitment to 4 million young people across the U.S. achieving economic mobility by 2030. Progress in StriveTogether’s Theory of Action™

RMP is proud to be one of nearly 70 partnerships within the national StriveTogether Cradle to Career network and the only one in Colorado. Their ambitious goal is to place 4 million more young people (up to age 35) on a path to economic mobility by 2030.

About

About the development of this scorecard

The development of the Rocky Mountain Partnership Cradle to Career™ Data Scorecard is rooted in the Results-Based Accountability™ framework. This Scorecard is intended to be a tool to help the RMP Region make tangible improvements to Cradle to Career Outcomes by focusing on clear goals and using data to track progress and impact.

We recognize that there are many other data sources, tools, and information that already exist, and this Data Scorecard works to leverage those existing assets and make them easier to actionize, rather than duplicate efforts or recreate the wheel. This includes ensuring alignment with other local, regional, state, and national stakeholders and agencies.

About the scorecard

826,000+

Across Colorado, more than 826,000 young people up to age 35 are not yet meeting key cradle-to-career milestones that are critical for long-term success and upward mobility and economic vitality.

To help close this gap, Rocky Mountain Partnership Cradle to Career™ (RMP) has committed to supporting 71,142 more young people in the north metro region (Adams County, City of Arvada, City of Aurora, City & County of Broomfield) to meet these milestones by 2030.

The Scorecard provides a clear, consistent, and shared view of how the north metro region is doing, and how much progress the RMP network is making towards its 71K goal.

While the milestones tracked may appear education-focused, the factors that most impact whether young people meet them go far beyond the classroom. This tool also includes key indicators that reflect the broader conditions of our communities, like housing, health, and access to opportunity, and how these impact upward mobility and economic vitality.

Whether you’re a policymaker, educator, employer, funder, or community member, this tool is designed to guide and support your work to improve outcomes for young people in the north metro region.

Data values

Below are RMP’s values when it comes to using data, community voice and perspective, and other information to tell the story of what’s happening in our region and how the region is working to change it.

  1. 01Use data as a flashlight, not a hammer
  2. 02Disaggregate data to understand who / where in the region is most impacted
  3. 03Use an asset-framing approach
  4. 04Tell the story behind the numbers
  5. 05Use data to drive action, and to continuously improve
  6. 06Define the questions we need answers to, and use data to help improve them
  7. 07Build on what works
  8. 08Align with local, state, and national entities
  9. 09Use the best available data, not letting perfect be the enemy of progress
  10. 10Keep the data timely and relevant
  11. 11Source from reliable and trusted entities
  12. 12Make data public: data belongs to everyone

Part of a statewide networkDraft

Regions across Colorado are working together toward one shared goal: 162,000 more young people meeting Cradle to Career milestones by 2030.
